## Summary
Model Aviation article proposing a CG and tail-sizing method for RC model airplanes that avoids percent-of-MAC targets. It argues that %MAC is unreliable and instead ties CG to a constant times the square root of wing area. It gives no percent-MAC static margin range.

## Usable rules / numbers
- [static_margin] No percent-of-MAC or percent-of-chord static margin range is given. The article argues %MAC is an unreliable basis (section "NEW METHOD").
- [static_margin] Its CG method uses a constant C10 = 0.0429 times the square root of wing area in inches, described as a starting point; it says it would never go below C6 = 0.0257 (section "NEW METHOD").
- [static_margin] For flying wings (tailless), it says to base C10 on total wing area including elevons (section "TAIL SIZE").

## How we could use it
- Does not confirm or change the 5 to 15 percent MAC rule; it is a counterpoint showing that %MAC targets are not universal in model practice.
- Relevant if the validator ever moves to an area-based CG check for small models.
