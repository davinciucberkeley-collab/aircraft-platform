## What's novel
A second homebuilt electric ultralight that drops the boxy fuselage of the MK.1 for a more two-dimensional aluminium structure. The article says the MK.1 weighed about 230 lb and flew about 15 minutes. The MK.2 target was an hour of flight.

## How it was built
- The structure uses aluminium members cut from hardware-store stock, with the landing gear machined from 1/4 in aluminum.
- Tail surfaces are foam covered with fibreglass.
- Tail controls run from a joystick through a pushrod and bellcrank to the surfaces.
- The article says the aircraft went from concept to finished machine in about one month.

## Problems and fixes
- The planned pulley system for the tail controls put too much load on the joystick. The builder swapped in a pushrod and bellcrank.
- Low-speed handling was worse than on the MK.1, possibly because the MK.1 had two wings that gained from ground effect. The article does not resolve this.
- The landing gear bent on the last test hop. It was straightened, not fully.
- More power (larger motors and props) was on order.

## Lessons for us
- A pushrod with a bellcrank was a simple fix for control loads that a pulley could not carry.
- Ground testing and short hops came before real flights. Check low-speed handling before adding power.
