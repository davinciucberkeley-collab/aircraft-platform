## What's novel
A ring-shaped (annular) wing for a powered model. In a small rubber-band test the ring wing stayed aloft longer than a straight wing. The full-size version uses two electric motors mounted high on the aircraft.

## How it was built
- The design was done in CAD, and the parts were laser-cut from balsa and assembled like a kit.
- The fuselage is a long box with reinforced bulkheads.
- Later versions use foam and aluminum tube for the wing, covered in heat-shrink film, with a central strut added to the final ring.

## Problems and fixes
- Early powered launches pulled the nose down and snapped the spars in collisions.
- A second attempt used the rear ring as the main wing with a conventional tail. It had the same propulsion problem.
- Gliding needed a nose-heavy balance and a steep angle of attack.
- The final wing bent rather than broke and flew stably.

## Lessons for us
- A ring wing can be built with cheap laser-cut parts, but the spars need to be sized for impact, not just for flight loads.
- Testing with rubber-band models first gave a cheap comparison of lift before the large wing was built.
