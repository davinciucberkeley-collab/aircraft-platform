## What's novel
A scratch-built Bixler-style foam plane where the wings are cut with a bow hot wire from paper or cardboard templates and the fuselage with a scroll-saw-style hot wire. Very cheap foam, carbon rods, and tape carry the structure.

## How it was built
- Wings: a 55 to 60 cm hot-wire bow runs along one template on each side of the foam block; the author says two people make it easier.
- Fuselage: a separate scroll-saw-style hot wire cutter, based on a design credited to a named online plan.
- Spar: carbon rods are seated in a slot about 6.5 cm back from the leading edge and glued.
- Joints: wing to winglets, wing into a 2.5 cm fuselage slot, tail glued on, control horns glued onto beveled hinges, servos set into cut holes.
- Skin: strapping tape over the whole fuselage. Hot glue and super glue are advised against for the foam.

## Problems and fixes
- Tail-heavy at first flight: fixed by adding weight under the nose.
- Not stable in flight (commenter and author agree): author suggests rebuilding in XPS foam; no rebuild is reported.

## Lessons for us
- The build relies on cheap cardboard templates and EPS foam; the article gives no total or cutter cost to compare.
- The article's own stability complaint points to foam choice and balance as the things to check on a cheap foam wing.
