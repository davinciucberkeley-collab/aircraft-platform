## What's novel
A foamboard trainer kit-style build aimed at a budget headline of "under $30," with the parts list split into foam-board cost and electronics cost. The post is mostly a repair-and-tuning guide, which makes the build easy to recover after crashes.

## How it was built
- Cut by scoring deeply and then snapping the foam; cut with paper side up; edges sanded with 220-grit paper.
- Fuselage sides and formers are joined with hot glue. The wing panels are joined at a glued center joint.
- Triangular stock reinforces fuselage joints. Spars are embedded in the wing.
- Packing tape is run diagonally over the wing for stiffness.
- Motor sits on a plywood firewall; servos go in wing bays and the fuselage; the battery rides on a Velcro tray.

## Problems and fixes
- Dives: add up-elevator trim or move the battery forward.
- Climbs: add down-elevator trim or move the battery back.
- Roll to one side: adjust aileron trim or check motor thrust angle.
- Wing rock: increase dihedral or check wing alignment.
- Cracks: reinforce with packing tape on both sides.
- Broken fuselage: hot-glue it back together and add triangular stock.
- Crushed nose: cut away the damage and rebuild.
- Wing damage: splint with carbon fiber or bamboo skewers.

## Lessons for us
- Stated figures are only partial: foam-board items are listed at $3.75 to $6.25 and electronics at $15 to $20, with no exact total.
- The post suggests ironing warped foam, which the author does not test and which carries a burn risk.
