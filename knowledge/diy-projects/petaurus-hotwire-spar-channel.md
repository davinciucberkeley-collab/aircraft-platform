## What's novel
A hot-wire wing core that also cuts a cylindrical channel for a carbon spar, using a bolted wooden frame, a spring-tensioned nichrome wire, and laser-cut plywood blanks as guides. The spar channel is cut by a sequence of wire passes rather than drilled.

## How it was built
- Nichrome wire is kept taut by a spring and chain on a three-piece wooden frame joined with M5 bolts. Power comes from a digital battery charger set to 6 A and 14 V.
- Plywood blanks are mounted on each end of the foam and aligned with a cardboard guide; pins go in perpendicular to the wire path.
- For the spar channel, a special blank with a circular cutout and an access slot is used; the slot is widened with a bandsaw, the wire is run through, and several passes around the perimeter free the cylinder.
- The carbon tube is seated in the channel; the planned aileron hinges are Kevlar and the planned skin is vacuum-bagged composite.
- Airfoil: NACA 6317.

## Problems and fixes
- Rough edges from hand-cut aluminum blanks: switched to laser-cut plywood blanks.
- Uneven cut at the far end: surface smoothed by hand.
- Debris stuck in the spar channel: a slow working method was found; author plans to speed it up.
- Spar fit so tight the foam might crack: inserted without cracking; author plans about 0.5 mm more clearance next time.

## Lessons for us
- Laser-cut blanks replaced hand-cut ones and fixed edge quality; this is a cheap, repeatable jig improvement.
- Plan clearance into the spar channel from the start, since a press fit risks cracking the foam.
- Wire safety: the spring-tensioned wire can release suddenly if an eye hook fails; wear eye protection.
