## What's novel
A delta built from two foamboard pieces, with a fuselage that folds and can swap onto other wings such as the Nutball and FT Flyer. The fold uses the KF airfoil step, and the page describes the fuselage as an interchangeable unit.

## How it was built
- Two pieces of Dollar Tree foam board form the wing and fuselage.
- A folding technique forms the airfoil step.
- Music wire forms the landing gear. Readers disagree on the wire gauge.
- The fuselage can be moved onto other swappable wings.

## Problems and fixes
- Plans mark the center of gravity with a circle and a cross. Some builders moved the balance point forward of that mark to improve handling.
- Print the plans at full size using a poster print with cut marks. Some tiled plans are missing the fin gauge and have a page-count mismatch.
- One prop came off because it was held by an O-ring, so an alternate attachment was suggested.
- Servos ran at different speeds, and one builder fixed it by adjusting transmitter settings.
- A reader says an ESC rated about 25 to 50 percent above motor current is the safer choice, and oversized props can overheat the motor.

## Lessons for us
- Mixed flight reports on a delta point to balance (CG) as the main setup risk, so mark and check the balance point before the first flight.
- Swappable fuselages add a fold step that needs accurate templates. Check tiled plans against the printed scale.
