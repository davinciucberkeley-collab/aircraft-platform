## What's novel
A flying-wing combat plane cut from corrugated plastic (coroplast) and glued with hot glue, using zip ties as elevon horns and a plain paint stick as the motor mount. The author calls the coroplast essentially free.

## How it was built
- Parts are cut from coroplast and glued together following linked plans; no added reinforcement.
- Elevon horns are large zip ties pushed through slots cut in the wing.
- Motor is glued straight in line with the wing (no offset, no thrust angle); motor mount is a paint stick.
- Radio is set to elevon mixing with large throws.
- Battery slot is cut to a friction fit in the fuselage.

## Problems and fixes
- Tears from combat: tape them and keep flying.
- Prop strike cut a servo wire: repaired in the field; salvaged parts reused for the next build.
- Stock fc2812 motor is underpowered: some pilots use a 2826-2200 motor; the author had good results rewinding the 2812 (9 turns of 24 AWG high-temp wire).
- Will not loop inverted: author suspects control setup, not confirmed.
- Flexes in flight and loses altitude in turns; still usable.

## Lessons for us
- Coroplast flexes enough that the author reports it still flies, but tears need taping; plan for field repair.
- Centre of gravity placement (ahead of the prop slot, battery just under the wing's front edge) is the setup the author gives.
